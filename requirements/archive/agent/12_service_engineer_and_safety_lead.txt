Differences from the plan below:
- Diagnosis on a ticket is started from the console, not by a copilot tool. It works on a
  diagnostic run: recorded motion replayed through the deployment pipeline on a copy of the twin
  with whatever was injected into the node's declared world. Every check it runs becomes a
  ticket record, and the FaultReport is written by code around the cause the model names.
- The calibration sweep is one action over all declared calibration poses under one approval
  by an operator, not one approval per pose. The engineer holds the scope for this one tool and
  no scope to move the arm otherwise.
- The camera is DECLARED: what it sees is computed from a declared transform with declared
  noise, and the camera move is INJECTED.
- Activating a calibration goes through the same proposal record and the same two-person rule
  as a configuration patch, as a change of kind "calibration".
- Site events: limits_changed was added; a calibration change records calibration_redone; any
  other configuration change records software_updated.
- First run kept in verification/t12_first_run.json: the grounding guard refused two correct
  answers because the unit was not at the end of the key that carried the number, and refused
  values the engineer had stated in the request; and the copilot gave a ticket number where an
  evidence record id was needed. Tool results now carry the unit in the key, values stated in
  the request may be repeated, and the prompt says what an evidence id is.
Further limits: one diagnostic run per ticket; the affine correction is stored with the record
but the pre-flight residual check uses the rigid transform only.

Job
---
Give the service engineer a ticket queue, the evidence behind each ticket, diagnosis and
calibration procedures, and a way to propose a change. Give the safety lead the queue of
proposed changes and overridden verdicts. Keep the two apart: the person who proposes a change
is never the person who approves it.

Requirements
------------
Service engineer
R1  Ticket queue for their site: symptom, source (operator, failed pre-flight, failed trial),
    status, and the incident finding when one exists (task 08).
R2  Service toolset: everything the operator can observe; raw configuration read; calibration
    records and residuals; the diagnostic tools of task 04; search of documentation and support
    threads, and search by log line; the service runbooks (calibration_validity,
    collect_calibration_samples, instrument_change); propose_config_patch; ticket tools.
R3  Diagnosis. The engineer can start the "eliminate" strategy of task 04 or the triage team of
    task 08 on a ticket. The result is attached to the ticket as a FaultReport with resolvable
    evidence.
R4  Calibration procedure. collect_calibration_samples moves the arm through declared poses
    (operator approval applies) and records samples. The fit is done by code: a rigid fit and
    the local affine correction, with residuals on held-out samples. The result is a draft
    CalibrationRecord; it becomes active only by an approved change.
R5  Change proposal. A ConfigPatch (task 02) with the reason, the evidence, and the effect on the
    site's named poses computed on the twin.
R6  Service report. On closing a ticket a structured report is generated from the ticket's
    records: symptom, finding, change applied, verification run, and what was not verified. It
    must pass the grounding rules against those records.
R7  The service engineer cannot approve a change, cannot activate a calibration, and cannot run
    a trial for the record.

Safety lead
R8  Change queue. Each proposal shows the patch, its validation result, the difference on the
    twin, the proposer and the evidence. Approve or reject, with a reason.
R9  Two-person rule. The approver must be a different principal from the proposer; the check is
    on the server. A proposal that loosens a limit is marked and needs a written reason.
R10 Applying a change records an event (software_updated, calibration_changed, limits_changed)
    that ends the validity of the site memory it affects (task 06 R12) and of open approvals.
R11 Override review. Trial verdicts overridden by an operator are listed with both verdicts.
R12 Support grants. The safety lead grants and revokes remote-support access (task 13 R6).
R13 The safety lead has no agent that can act. Their copilot is read-only: it explains a
    proposal and retrieves what the documentation says about the changed keys.

Verification on real data (planned)
-----------------------------------
python scripts/verify.py t12    ->  verification/t12_service_access.json
  a. A ticket from a failed pre-flight to closure: diagnosis, calibration samples, fit and
     held-out residuals against the injected offset, proposal, approval by the safety lead,
     activation, pre-flight passing, service report valid or not and attempts.
  b. Questions through the chat path: a real error line searched in the support threads; a
     configuration key explained from the schema and documentation; a comparison of two
     calibration records.
  c. Two-person rule: proposer approving own change; another engineer approving; safety lead
     approving. Loosened limit without a reason.
  d. After activation: the old calibration fact reported as expired; an approval issued before
     the change refused.
Browser check: ticket and change pages by role; operator and remote support refused.

Known limits
------------
The calibration fit is exercised on generated samples, so it shows the procedure and the
bookkeeping, not accuracy on hardware. One safety lead per site; no delegation or absence rule.
